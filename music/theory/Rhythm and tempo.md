# Rhythm and tempo

#music/rhythm · Note 7 of 9 · part of [[Guitar]]

Wrong notes in time sound like a choice. Right notes out of time sound like a
mistake. Rhythm is the half that gets neglected.

## Tempo is beats per minute

**BPM** — how many beats fit in a minute. 60 BPM is one beat a second.

The Italian words are ranges, not precise values:

| Marking | BPM | Feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Largo | 40–60 | very slow |
| Adagio | 66–76 | slow |
| Andante | 76–108 | walking pace |
| Moderato | 108–120 | moderate |
| Allegro | 120–156 | brisk |
| Presto | 168–200 | fast |

Most songs live between 70 and 140. A metronome is not optional equipment.

## Note values

Each is half the one before:

| Note | British name | Beats in 4/4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 𝅝 whole | semibreve | 4 |
| 𝅗𝅥 half | minim | 2 |
| ♩ quarter | crotchet | 1 |
| ♪ eighth | quaver | ½ |
| 𝅘𝅥𝅯 sixteenth | semiquaver | ¼ |

A **dot** adds half the note's own value again: a dotted half = 3 beats. A
**tie** joins two notes into one longer sound across a bar line.

## Time signatures

Written as a fraction at the start of the staff:

- **top number** — beats in a bar
- **bottom number** — which note value gets one beat (4 = quarter, 8 = eighth)

| Signature | Beats | Count | Where you hear it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4/4** | 4 quarters | 1 2 3 4 | almost everything |
| **3/4** | 3 quarters | 1 2 3 | waltz |
| **6/8** | 6 eighths | 1 2 3 4 5 6 | felt in two, lilting |
| **2/4** | 2 quarters | 1 2 | marches |
| **5/4** | 5 quarters | 1 2 3 4 5 | odd, deliberate |

4/4 is so common it is written **C** on the staff — for *common time*, not for C.

## The same idea in taal

Hindustani music counts in **taal**, cycles of **matras** (beats) grouped into
**vibhags** (sections). The **sam** is beat one — the point everything resolves to.

| Taal | Matras | Grouping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Teentaal** | 16 | 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 |
| **Keherwa** | 8 | 4 + 4 |
| **Dadra** | 6 | 3 + 3 |
| **Ektaal** | 12 | 2+2+2+2+2+2 |
| **Jhaptaal** | 10 | 2 + 3 + 2 + 3 |

Keherwa maps cleanly onto 4/4, Dadra onto 6/8. Teentaal is four bars of 4/4 —
but the *emphasis* differs: Western 4/4 stresses beats 1 and 3, while a taal
cycle points everything at the sam.

Jhaptaal's 2+3+2+3 has no comfortable Western equivalent, which is a good
reminder that the mapping is a convenience, not an identity.

## Strumming, written down

**D** = down, **U** = up. Count "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and", downs on the numbers,
ups on the "and".

```
1  &  2  &  3  &  4  &
D     D  U     U  D  U        ← the one that fits most songs
D  U  D  U  D  U  D  U        ← constant eighths
D     D     D     D           ← quarters, for learning changes
```

Keep the hand moving continuously even when not striking. The pattern comes from
which strokes you *miss*, not from starting and stopping — that is the single
thing that makes strumming sound relaxed instead of stiff.

## How to practise with a metronome

1. Set it **slower than feels necessary**. 60 BPM.
2. Play until four bars are perfectly clean.
3. Raise by 5 BPM.
4. When it falls apart, drop 10 and stay there.

Speed built this way holds up. Speed rushed does not.
