# Sargam and the scale

#music/sargam · Note 2 of 9 · part of [[Guitar]]

## The seven swaras

**Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni** — then Sa again, an octave up. Written in full:
Shadja, Rishabh, Gandhar, Madhyam, Pancham, Dhaivat, Nishad. Nobody says that.

They map onto Western solfège exactly:

| Sargam | Solfège | In C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sa | Do | C |
| Re | Re | D |
| Ga | Mi | E |
| Ma | Fa | F |
| Pa | Sol | G |
| Dha | La | A |
| Ni | Ti | B |
| Sa' | Do | C (octave up) |

> [!important] Sa is not C
> Sa is *home*, not a pitch. The table above assumes you chose C. Choose D and
> Re becomes E, Ga becomes F♯, and everything still sounds the same, just higher.
> This is why the fret shape below works everywhere.

## The gaps are not equal

Count semitones from Sa:

| | Sa | Re | Ga | Ma | Pa | Dha | Ni | Sa' |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Semitones from Sa** | 0 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 11 | 12 |
| **Step to the next** | 2 | 2 | **1** | 2 | 2 | 2 | **1** | |

Whole, whole, **half**, whole, whole, whole, **half**. That is the major scale —
and in Hindustani terms, **Bilawal thaat**, the one where every swara is
*shuddha* (natural).

The two half steps, Ga→Ma and Ni→Sa', are where E–F and B–C sit on a piano.

## All twelve, with the altered swaras

**Sa and Pa never change.** They are *achal* — immovable. Everything else has an
alternative:

| Semitones | Sargam | Western (in C) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Sa | C |
| 1 | komal Re | D♭ |
| 2 | Re | D |
| 3 | komal Ga | E♭ |
| 4 | Ga | E |
| 5 | Ma | F |
| 6 | **tivra** Ma | F♯ |
| 7 | Pa | G |
| 8 | komal Dha | A♭ |
| 9 | Dha | A |
| 10 | komal Ni | B♭ |
| 11 | Ni | B |
| 12 | Sa' | C |

*Komal* = flattened. *Tivra* = sharpened, and only Ma gets it. Four komal swaras
plus one tivra plus the seven shuddha gives the twelve semitones of the octave —
the same twelve frets from [[The fretboard]].

## On one string

Because a fret is a semitone, the whole scale is just those numbers. Pick any
fret as Sa and count:

```
Sa   Re   Ga   Ma   Pa   Dha  Ni   Sa'
+0   +2   +4   +5   +7   +9   +11  +12
```

On the 5th string, 3rd fret = C. Take that as Sa:

```
A|--3---5---7---8---10--12--14--15--
    Sa  Re  Ga  Ma  Pa  Dha Ni  Sa'
```

Move the whole thing to the 5th fret and Sa is D. Nothing else changes. **That
is the entire payoff of a movable system.**

## Across the strings — the shape worth memorising

One string is impractical. This two-string, two-octave box is the one to learn,
with **Sa on the 6th string**:

```
e|------------------------------
B|------------------------------
G|------------------------------
D|--------------2--4--5--7---  Pa Dha Ni Sa'
A|--------2--3-----------------    Re  Ga
E|--0--------------------------    Sa
```

With Sa at the 6th string 8th fret (C), that is C major. Slide it to fret 10 and
it is D major. The shape never changes — which is exactly the point.

> [!warning] The 2nd string breaks the shape
> Any pattern crossing to the B string needs **one extra fret**, because G→B is
> a major third rather than a fourth ([[The fretboard]]). Everyone
> forgets this. Everyone plays a wrong note the first few times.

## Aroha and avaroha

Ascending is **aroha**, descending **avaroha**. Practise both — descending is
harder and is where melodies actually resolve.

A raga is largely defined by which swaras it uses going up versus coming down,
and which it lingers on. That is well past this note, but it is why the same
seven notes produce hundreds of distinct moods.
