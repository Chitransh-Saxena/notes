# Guitar — A melody, worked through

#music/melody · Note 9 of 9 · part of [[Guitar]]

Everything in this series, applied to eight bars. The melody is my own, written
to be simple enough to analyse and long enough to have a shape.

**Key:** C major — Sa = C · **Time:** 4/4 · **Tempo:** ♩= 90, andante

## The melody in sargam

```
│ Sa  Re  Ga  Ma │ Pa  –   Pa  –  │ Dha Pa  Ma  Ga │ Re  –   –   –  │
│ Ga  Ma  Pa  Dha│ Ni  –   Ni  –  │ Sa' Ni  Dha Pa │ Sa  –   –   –  │
```

Each `–` holds the note before it for another beat. Bars 4 and 8 are a single
note held for four.

## Why it works

**Bars 1–4 climb and fall back.** Sa up to Pa, a rest on Pa, then down to Re.
Landing on Re is deliberately unfinished — Re is the second degree, and the ear
expects it to keep moving. That tension is what carries you into bar 5.

**Bars 5–8 do it again, higher, and resolve.** The same rising shape starting a
third up, reaching Ni — the leading note, the most restless of all — and only
then falling to Sa. Ni to Sa' is a semitone, the smallest step available, and
that is precisely why the ending sounds like an ending.

**The arch.** Lowest note Sa in bar 1, highest Sa' in bar 7, home in bar 8. One
rise and one release across eight bars, which is roughly the smallest structure
that feels like a complete thought.

## Chords underneath

Harmonise it with the scale's own triads ([[Guitar — Chords]]):

```
│ C      │ C      │ F      │ G      │
│ C      │ G      │ Am     │ C      │
```

That is **I – I – IV – V – I – V – vi – I**. The G under bar 4 is the important
one: the V chord under a held Re makes the unfinished feeling deliberate rather
than accidental.

## How to practise it

1. **Sing it in sargam first**, no guitar. If you cannot sing it you cannot
   phrase it.
2. Play at **60 BPM** with a metronome, one bar at a time.
3. Join bars in pairs, then fours, then all eight.
4. Bring it to **90 BPM** only when eight bars are clean twice in a row.
5. Add the chords last — or record yourself playing them and play the melody
   over the top.

Then move Sa. Put it at the 5th fret of the 5th string and the whole thing is in
D, with the same shapes and the same fingering. Or clamp a capo at fret 2 and
play it exactly as written — same result, no thinking required
([[Guitar — The capo]]). That transposition, done without effort, is the point of
everything in this series.
