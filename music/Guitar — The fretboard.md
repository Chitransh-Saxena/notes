# Guitar — The fretboard

#music/fretboard · Note 1 of 6 · part of [[Guitar]]

## Six strings

In standard tuning, from the thickest string to the thinnest:

| String | Note | Also called |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6th | **E** | low E |
| 5th | **A** | |
| 4th | **D** | |
| 3rd | **G** | |
| 2nd | **B** | |
| 1st | **E** | high E |

The 6th string is the one nearest your face when playing; the 1st is nearest the
floor. Confusingly, "up" in pitch means "down" toward the floor.

**E A D G B E.** Say it until it is automatic — every diagram in every note
after this assumes it.

## One fret is one semitone

Every fret is a **half step**. Twelve frets is a **full octave** — the same note
again, at double the frequency.

So the 12th fret of any string is that string's open note, an octave up. The
fretboard then repeats: fret 13 is the same as fret 1, an octave higher.

The twelve notes, ascending:

```
C  C♯/D♭  D  D♯/E♭  E  F  F♯/G♭  G  G♯/A♭  A  A♯/B♭  B  → C
```

**There is no black key between E–F and B–C.** They are a semitone apart
naturally. This single fact explains most of the awkwardness in music theory,
and it is why the major scale is not evenly spaced.

## The tuning is *nearly* regular

Count the frets between adjacent strings:

| From → to | Frets | Interval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E → A | 5 | perfect 4th |
| A → D | 5 | perfect 4th |
| D → G | 5 | perfect 4th |
| **G → B** | **4** | **major 3rd** |
| B → E | 5 | perfect 4th |

Five of the six are a fourth apart, and then **G to B breaks the pattern**.

This is the reason chord shapes look irregular, why a shape moved across the
strings does not sound the same, and why the 2nd string always needs a mental
adjustment. It is not you. It is the tuning, and it is a deliberate compromise
that makes open chords easier at the cost of symmetry.

## Finding any note

Two anchors are enough to never be lost:

- **6th string**: fret 3 = G, fret 5 = A, fret 7 = B, fret 8 = C, fret 12 = E
- **5th string**: fret 3 = C, fret 5 = D, fret 7 = E, fret 10 = G, fret 12 = A

Then two shortcuts:

- **Same note, two strings across, two frets up** — except when the 2nd string
  is involved, where it is three frets. (That G–B third again.)
- **Octave up**: two strings toward the floor, two frets up.

## Practise this, not scales

Set a timer for two minutes. Name a note, find it on all six strings. That
exercise is worth more than a month of scale patterns, because everything in
[[Guitar — Sargam and the scale]] and [[Guitar — Chords]] assumes you can.
