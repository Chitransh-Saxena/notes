# Photo — Low light

#photo/lowlight · Note 3 of 7 · part of [[Photography]]

Everything here follows from one fact: **the R50 has no in-body stabilisation.**
All the stabilising comes from the lens, and the RF 35mm f/1.8 has good Hybrid IS
— roughly five stops. So:

> **Static subjects: excellent. Moving subjects: ordinary.**
> IS stabilises the camera. It has never once stabilised a subject.

## Static subject — a room, a street, a still life

IS is doing the work, so the shutter floor drops a long way below the usual
1/60 from [[Photo — Exposure, plainly]]:

- **f/1.8**
- **1/15s, or slower** — I have had sharp frames at 1/8s braced against a wall
- **ISO 800–3200**

Brace against something. Breathe out, then shoot. Take three; one will be sharp.

## Moving subject — people, a room with life in it

IS is now irrelevant and the shutter has to do it alone:

- **f/1.8**
- **1/125s** minimum for someone talking; **1/250s** if they are walking
- **ISO 3200–6400**, and accept it

This is the trade nobody likes: you cannot have a sharp moving subject in a dark
room at low ISO. Something has to give, and it should be the ISO — noise is
recoverable, motion blur is not.

## The Auto ISO setup I actually use

- Mode: **Av** at f/1.8
- **Auto ISO**, ceiling **6400**
- **Minimum shutter speed** set manually — 1/60 for still scenes, 1/160 when
  there are people in the frame

Two dial changes cover almost everything.

## Noise is a solved problem, mostly

Shoot **RAW**. Modern denoise — Lightroom's AI Denoise, DxO PureRAW, Topaz — takes
ISO 6400 to something that looks like ISO 1600, and it is genuinely remarkable.
It cannot invent detail that motion blur destroyed.

This is why the rule is *raise ISO, protect shutter speed*.

## Focus in the dark

The R50's Dual Pixel AF is very good in low light, but at f/1.8 up close the
depth of field is millimetres. Two habits:

- **Eye detection on**, always, for people
- **One Shot** for static subjects, **Servo** the moment anything moves

If AF hunts, it is usually a contrast problem — aim at an edge, not a flat wall.

## When to give up and use a tripod

If the subject is not moving, a tripod removes the entire problem: ISO 100,
f/5.6, whatever shutter you like. That is also the doorway to
[[Photo — Night sky]].

**Turn IS off on a tripod.** A stabiliser with nothing to correct can hunt
against itself and soften the frame.
