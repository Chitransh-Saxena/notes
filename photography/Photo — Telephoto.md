# Photo — Telephoto

#photo/tele · Note 5 of 7 · part of [[Photography]]

I do not own one yet. This is the note for when I do.

## What the 35mm cannot reach

At ~56mm equivalent ([[Photo — The kit]]), everything distant stays distant.
No wildlife, no candid street from across a road, no moon bigger than a dot, no
compression — that flattened, stacked look that only long lenses give.

## What I would buy

| Lens | Reach on APS-C | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RF-S 55–210mm f/5–7.1 IS STM** | ~88–336mm eq | The sensible first one. Light, cheap, stabilised, made for this body |
| **RF 100–400mm f/5.6–8 IS USM** | ~160–640mm eq | Enormous reach for the money. The birds-and-moon lens |
| **RF 70–200mm f/4L IS USM** | ~112–320mm eq | Optically superb, constant f/4, several times the price |

**The 55–210 first.** It costs little, weighs nothing, and will tell me within a
month whether I actually shoot long or just like the idea of it.

The catch on all of them: **they are slow**. f/7.1 at the long end is roughly
four stops darker than the 35mm at f/1.8. Telephoto is a daylight activity unless
there is a tripod involved.

## Technique, which changes completely

**Shutter speed.** The 1/(focal × 1.6) rule bites hard here. At 210mm that is
1/336 → round to **1/500**. For birds in flight, 1/1000 to 1/2000. IS helps with
shake, not with a bird.

**Stability.** Long lenses magnify every tremor. Support the barrel, not the body.
Tuck elbows in. A monopod is transformative and packs down small.

**Depth of field is thinner than you expect.** At 210mm and f/7.1, focus is still
critical — reach compresses depth as well as distance.

**Atmosphere is the real limit.** Heat shimmer over a long distance destroys
detail no lens can recover. Shoot early, shoot across cool ground.

## The moon, specifically

The moon is a sunlit rock, so it is far brighter than instinct suggests. Metering
for the black sky around it is what blows it out to a white disc.

- **Manual mode**
- **f/8**, **ISO 100**, **1/125–1/250s**
- Manual focus, live view zoomed to 10×
- Spot metering if you stay in auto

At 400mm on APS-C (~640mm eq) the moon fills a useful part of the frame. At 210mm
it is small but croppable from 24MP.
