# Photo — Exposure, plainly

#photo/basics · Note 2 of 7 · part of [[Photography]]

Three controls, and each one costs you something other than brightness. That
second effect is the entire craft.

| Control | Brightness | What it *actually* costs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Aperture** (f/1.8 → f/16) | wider = brighter | depth of field, and corner sharpness |
| **Shutter** (1/1000 → 1s) | slower = brighter | motion blur — subject *and* camera |
| **ISO** (100 → 32000) | higher = brighter | noise, and dynamic range |

## Aperture

Smaller f-number = wider opening = more light and **less** in focus.

- **f/1.8** — maximum light, paper-thin focus. At close range on the 35mm, you
  can miss focus between an eye and an eyelash.
- **f/2.8–f/4** — a usable compromise; the lens is also getting sharper.
- **f/5.6** — about the sharpest this lens gets. See [[Photo — Peak sharpness]].
- **f/11+** — diffraction starts softening everything. On 24MP APS-C the losses
  are visible past roughly f/8–f/11. Stopping down further does not "add" sharpness.

## Shutter

The rule of thumb for handheld sharpness is **1 / (focal length × crop factor)**.
For the 35mm on the R50: 1/(35 × 1.6) ≈ **1/56**, so round to **1/60**.

That is the floor for *camera* shake with IS off. Subject motion is separate and
usually stricter:

- Someone standing still: 1/60
- Someone talking, gesturing: 1/125
- Walking: 1/250
- Kids, dogs, sport: 1/500 and up

The lens's IS buys you several stops **on the first number only**. It does
nothing whatsoever for the second.

## ISO

Raise it last, but raise it without guilt. A sharp, noisy photograph is a
photograph; a clean, blurred one is not.

On the R50, roughly:

- **100–800** — clean, use freely
- **1600–3200** — very usable, especially from RAW
- **6400** — usable, wants noise reduction
- **12800+** — emergencies, and modern AI denoise makes them surprisingly viable

## The order I actually set them

1. **Aperture** first — it is a *creative* choice about what is in focus.
2. **Shutter** second — set the floor the subject demands.
3. **ISO** last — let Auto ISO fill the gap, with a ceiling set.

In practice that is **Aperture Priority (Av)** with a minimum shutter speed and
an Auto ISO ceiling of 6400. That covers most of what I shoot without touching
a dial.
